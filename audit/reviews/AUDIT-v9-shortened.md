## The shared rank estimate

**EVIDENCE.** In Theorem 12, write $n_i=[A:K_i]$ and $c_{ij}=[A:K_iK_j]$ for an abelian group acting faithfully. On orbit $j$, the stabilizer $K_i$ has orbits of odd size $n_j/c_{ij}$, and the off-diagonal signed sum in row $i$ has absolute value at least that size. Skewness and $S^TS=vI-J$ give $\sum_j(n_i/n_j)Q_{ij}^2=v-n_i$. For $n_i>1$, inserting the lower bounds and the artificial diagonal contribution 1 gives $n_i\sum_j n_j/c_{ij}^2\le v-n_i+1<v$. Weighted AM–GM gives $2\sum_j(n_j/v)\log c_{ij}>\log n_i$. Apply the non-strict induction bound to the proper subgroup $K_i$, whose orbit on $j$ has size $n_j/c_{ij}$, and use $[A:K_i]=n_i$. The identity subgroup supplies the base. For equal or bounded orbit size $\ell^e$ the resulting strict bound $|A|<\ell^{2e}$ gives Corollaries 13–14, including $e=1$. This chain uses no plane lemma.

## Corollary 16

**CLAIM and EVIDENCE.** A normal $\ell$-group with orbits of prime size $\ell$ has order $\ell$. A transitive image on each orbit has order divisible by $\ell$, and $\nu_\ell(\ell!)=1$. Each image is therefore cyclic of order $\ell$. The product action is faithful, so the group is elementary abelian. Corollary 13 bounds its order above by $\ell$, and any one of its orbits bounds it below by $\ell$. This also handles a single orbit. The relative clause about order in the source refers to the transitive subgroup of the symmetric group.

## Proposition 17

**CLAIM and EVIDENCE.** For $r\ne p$ and normal elementary abelian $U$ with orbit size $r^2$, all stabilizers are lines in a three-dimensional space and all lines occur equally often. Orbit-stabilizer gives codimension two. Proposition 11(2) applies to every element of $U$, so the union of these stabilizers is $U$. Dimension two would make every stabilizer zero. Corollary 14 with $e=2$ gives dimension at most three. A nonzero vector on any line must be covered, hence that line occurs as a stabilizer. Normality of $U$ and vertex transitivity conjugate the orbit stabilizers transitively, with equal fibre sizes on the set of orbits. The number of lines is $(r^3-1)/(r-1)$, so it divides $v/r^2$. **Coverage.** The case $r=p$ cannot use the covering argument and is expressly excluded.

## Lemma 23

**CLAIM and EVIDENCE.** Write $N=\Omega_1(Z(P))$. Characteristicity, transitivity and faithfulness force its common orbit size to be $p$ or $p^2$. In the latter case its orbits equal those of $P$. Its regular abelian image on each orbit is its own centralizer by Lemma 5. The larger image of $P$ both contains it and centralizes it. Thus $P$ embeds in a product of elementary abelian images, and Corollary 14 gives rank at most three.

In the former case Corollary 16 gives $|N|=p$. An abelian $P$ with one order-$p$ subgroup is cyclic. For nonabelian $P$, the kernel on $N$-orbits is normal in $G$, contains $N$ and has the same orbits as $N$. Corollary 16 makes this kernel $N$ itself. On each orbit of blocks, $E=P/N$ has image of order $p$ in $S_p$ and is faithful on their union. Thus $E$ is elementary abelian. Now $1\ne[P,P]=N\le Z(P)$, and the commutator gives a nonzero alternating form on $E$. Any block stabilizer is a hyperplane. Its preimage centralizes the regular $N$ on that block, hence has abelian image. Its commutators lie in $N$ and fix the block pointwise, hence are trivial. Each hyperplane is isotropic. Its codimension bounds the ambient alternating rank by two, and nonzero rank is even. Its image in the two-dimensional symplectic quotient has dimension at most one, so it contains the radical. The stabilizers intersect trivially by faithfulness, forcing radical zero and $\dim E=2$. Finally $[P,P]\le\Phi(P)\le N$ because $P/N$ is elementary abelian. All three branches give the stated bound. The deleted Fourier argument covered only the elementary abelian case.

## Theorem 37

**CLAIM and EVIDENCE.** Under the mixed-degree Fitting hypothesis the endpoint $p=2k-1$ is impossible. Theorem 35 offers $p\le n$ or $p\le2k-2$, so at equality $p=n=2e-1=2k-1$. Lemma 31 forces $D=Z$ there. Lemma 32 gives faithful action on the composition factors. Lemma 27 supplies an order-$p$ element, so some irreducible image has such an element. Its normal $p$-core acts trivially, and Theorem 28 forces dimension at least $p$. Thus $D$ is irreducible, $P$ centralizes it, and their identical orbits and Lemma 5 make $P$ elementary abelian. Corollary 14 now gives $P=D$. Its dual is irreducible.

Over the algebraic closure the Fitting subgroup of $H$ has coprime order. Its isotypic components are transitive under $H$ and Frobenius together, since a union of an orbit descends to an invariant rational subspace. Each constituent degree divides the prime dimension $p$ and is prime to $p$ by the Sylow product decomposition of the nilpotent group. Thus it is one. A single scalar component would centralize the Fitting subgroup in all of $H$, contrary to the $p$-part of $H$. There are $p$ weight lines. The diagonal kernel $H_0$ is abelian of order prime to $p$. A solvable transitive group of prime degree has a regular normal $C_p$ (a minimal abelian normal subgroup is transitive by primitivity, hence regular); the stabilizer embeds in its cyclic automorphism group. Consequently $\nu_p(|H|)=1$. If Frobenius cycles the lines, one eigenvalue determines every diagonal entry of an element of $H_0$, making $H_0$ cyclic. Otherwise all lines are defined over $\mathbb F_p$.

On $Y$, $|Y|=pd$ with $d=s^2>1$. A lift of a generator of the cyclic line-permutation point stabilizer has order prime to $p$ and fixes a point of $Y$ by Lemma 27. The number of $H_0$-orbits divides $p$ and is divisible by $p$, hence equals $p$. These orbits have size $d$ and their stabilizers cover $H_0$. Lemma 6 gives $d<p$ and excludes a cyclic $H_0$. Thus every prime dividing $d$ divides $p-1$. If $p-d=2$, any such prime divides both $d$ and $d+1$. Since $d$ is an odd square, $9\le d\le p-6$. Write $p=4m-1$, $k=2m$, $m\ge4$.

For the $N$ distinct annihilator types, irreducibility rules out $N=1$. The quotient law and transitivity give $N=p l\mid pd$, and each type occurs in $c=pd/N$ cells. Lemmas 7 and 9 give $m\le t=\dim(W\cap W')\le2m-1$ for distinct types: if $t<m$, a single squared row entry is at least $p^{k+2}$, above the full row norm. In the revised paragraph, dimension of intersection with a fixed type is supermodular by the dimension formula and inclusion of the sum of intersections in the intersection with the sum. Subtracting the linear dimension term preserves supermodularity. If two subspaces maximize $\psi$, their intersection and sum both maximize it. There are finitely many subspaces, so the sum of all maximizers is a maximizer and is $H$-invariant. Irreducibility and $\psi(0)=\psi(P^*)=0$ force the maximum to be zero. Substitution gives $\sum_{W'\ne W}(t-m)\le Nk^2/p-k-m(N-1)=(l-1)m$. This reconstructs the former reference to the removed 729p argument.

Convex interpolation of $p^{t-2m}$ at $m$ and $2m-1$, followed by the union bound, gives the displayed bound on the fraction belonging to another type. The first term is strictly below $p^{-2}$ because $N<p^2$, $m\ge4$. The other coefficient is at most $(p-7)m/(m-1)<p-3$. The private fraction therefore exceeds $3/p-p^{-2}>1/N$. Conversely the projection onto the private nontrivial characters is supported on the $c$ cells of this type, commutes with $S$, and projects a point mass in one cell to squared norm equal to the private fraction. On those characters $S^*S=vI$. The squared norm of the signed column restricted to the support is $cp^k-1=v/N-1$, giving a private fraction strictly below $1/N$. This is the contradiction. From $k\le2a$ and $p\equiv3\bmod4$, strictness gives $p\le4a-5$.
