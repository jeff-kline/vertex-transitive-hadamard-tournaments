## A stronger corollary for the introduction

**Every vertex-transitive Hadamard tournament of fourth-power-free order \(v>1\) is a Cayley tournament of a \(p\)-group.** Theorem 1 gives \(v=p\) or \(p^3\). Marušič proved that every vertex-transitive digraph on \(p^k\) vertices for \(k\le3\) is Cayley. Its regular group has order \(v\), and its out-neighbours of the identity form a skew Hadamard difference set. This proves Conjecture 13 throughout the range of the title theorem. The draft supplies the reduction to the two degrees. Marušič supplies Cayley representation at those degrees. [Marušič (1985)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-0208(08)73001-9)

## Why the cited prime-power metacirculants fail double regularity

Here is the specific test underlying the Conjecture 13 finding. Let a tournament have \(p^k\) vertices and an automorphism consisting of \(p\) cycles of common length \(n=p^{k-1}\). Whenever the degree is admissible for double regularity, \(p\equiv3\bmod4\) and \(k\) is odd. For \(k\ge5\) double regularity is impossible.

Indeed, take a primitive \(n\)-th root \(\zeta\). Its eigenspace for the cyclic permutation has dimension \(p\), one Fourier vector per cycle. This space is orthogonal to constants. If the tournament were doubly regular, its signed matrix on that space would square to \(-p^k I\). Its trace would be \(H p^{(k-1)/2}\sqrt{-p}\) for an odd nonzero integer \(H\), by the two eigenvalue multiplicities. On the other hand the diagonal circulant blocks give the trace \(F(\zeta)\), where the coefficient \(f_j\) of \(X^j\) in \(F\) is the sum of the \(p\) diagonal-block signs at offset \(j\). In particular \(|f_j|\le p\) and \(\deg F<n\).

Put \(d=n/p\). Up to sign, the quadratic Gauss sum \(\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}(\frac ap)\zeta^{ad}\) is \(\sqrt{-p}\). Subtract \(H p^{(k-1)/2}\) times the corresponding polynomial from \(F\), absorbing the overall sign into \(H\). The difference is divisible by \(\Phi_n(X)=\sum_{a=0}^{p-1}X^{ad}\). The quotient has degree \(<d\), so the coefficients of that difference at \(0,d,\ldots,(p-1)d\) are equal. Comparing a square and a nonsquare index gives \(2|H|p^{(k-1)/2}\le2p\), a contradiction.

Mansilla describes the prime-power family as metacirculants with parameters \(p^{k-1}\) and \(p\), \(k\ge4\). The order of the two parameters in the convention is immaterial. With \(p\) long blocks the rotation already has the required cycles. With \(p^{k-1}\) short blocks take the \(p\)-part of a block-cycling automorphism. Every vertex cycle then has length at least \(p^{k-1}\) and is a power of \(p\), so there are either \(p\) long cycles or one full cycle whose \(p\)-th power gives them. The even values of \(k\) have the wrong degree modulo 4. This checks the prime-power base family from the published description. Separately, any nontrivial lexicographic product of regular tournaments, with \(a\) outer vertices and \(b\) per fibre, fails double regularity. Two vertices in a single fibre already have \(b(a-1)/2>(ab-3)/4\) common out-neighbours outside it. We verified the published abstract of Mansilla; her full article was unavailable to this review. [Mansilla (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2004.08.002) · [Metacirculant definition](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10801-007-0107-y)
